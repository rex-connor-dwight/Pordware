When I walked into Bridge by Obsidian on the morning of September 26th, I wasn't entirely sure what to expect. I'd read the brief, seen the itinerary, even helped shape parts of it. But nothing quite prepares you for watching an idea like The Venture Room come to life in real time.

By 11:00am, founders were already trickling in. What struck me first wasn't the setup or the schedule, it was the energy. Founders greeted each other like old colleagues, even though many were meeting for the first time. There was none of the stiff, business-card-exchange energy typical of Lagos networking events. Just founders, genuinely curious about what everyone else in the room was building.

## Watching clarity happen in real time

When the Clarity session began, I expected some pushback from founders unused to having their ideas challenged so directly. Instead, I watched something different unfold, founders leaning in, taking notes, occasionally wincing but never shutting down. One thing became clear to me: people don't mind being challenged when they can tell it's coming from a place of genuinely wanting them to succeed.

## The elevator pitch moment

Perhaps the segment I found most revealing was the elevator pitch round. Ninety seconds. No slides. No safety net. I watched founders who'd clearly rehearsed polished pitch decks suddenly have to distill everything down to its rawest form. Some thrived under the pressure. Others visibly struggled, and in a way, that was the point. A pitch that only works with slides behind it isn't ready for a room like this one.

## Partnership and investment: substance over performance

As the day moved into Partnership and Investment, I noticed founders forming connections that felt organic rather than transactional, no one performing for an audience, no forced elevator small talk. Just founders talking through where their businesses genuinely overlapped, and where real collaboration might make sense.

## The open Q&A

By the time <a href="https://instagram.com/the.venture.architect" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Connor</a> opened the floor for direct Q&A, no panel, no moderation, just him and a room full of founders asking hard questions, I understood why the format mattered. It stripped away the performative layer that usually exists between founders and the people advising them. Just honest answers to honest questions.

## Walking away

By 4:00pm, as the room wound down for closing remarks, I found myself thinking less about the logistics I'd helped manage and more about what I'd actually witnessed: founders leaving sharper than they arrived. Not comforted, but clearer. And in a city full of noisy networking events, that felt like something worth building on.
